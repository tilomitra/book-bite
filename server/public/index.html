    What deep work is

    Newport defines deep work as professional activity performed in a state of distraction-free concentration, at the edge of one’s cognitive abilities. It creates value, improves skill, and is difficult to replicate.

    Its counterpart, shallow work, consists of logistical or low-demand tasks, such as routine email, scheduling and some meetings, that are often performed while distracted and are easy to reproduce.

    Shallow tasks may be necessary, but they can expand to fill the day, leaving little room for the work that requires thought and produces lasting results.

    Why it is rare

    Workplaces and technologies encourage constant connectivity, quick responses and frequent task switching. Being busy starts to look like being productive.
